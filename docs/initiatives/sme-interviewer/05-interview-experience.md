# Interview experience and behaviour

**Proposed conversation policy v0.1.** The goal is useful, accurate knowledge capture with a contributor who remains in control. Warmth is a style choice; transparency, provenance and the ability to correct are functional requirements.

## Session journey

| Stage | Participant experience | Behind the experience |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare | Topic, purpose, expected time, existing evidence and open questions are visible | Fetch eligible evidence pack; prepare coverage map and known uncertainties |
| Agree | Explain AI identity, recording/storage, review access and how to pause/correct | Explicit start; record agreed session settings; microphone inactive beforehand |
| Describe | “Walk me through a typical instance in your own words.” | Free-narrative mode; collect topics and gaps without frequent challenge |
| Explore | One question at a time about ownership, hand-offs, conditions and examples | Adaptive coverage tracking; distinguish knowledge gaps from model uncertainty |
| Clarify | Confirm unclear terminology, numbers or missing conditions | Resolve transcription before semantic conflict classification |
| Compare | Show a specific source and ask about the difference | Check applicability and evidence version; record both interpretations |
| Summarise | The SME corrects the process summary and sees remaining questions | Revision-aware claims; confirmation records intended meaning, not final approval |
| Review | Explain what was checked, what remains and who needs to decide | Frozen packet, validation jobs and owner-routed conflict cases |
| Return value | Provide reusable process draft, relevant evidence and follow-up list | No automatic knowledge publication or messaging |

A 20-minute session may reserve roughly 2 minutes for introduction, 6 for the account, 8 for focused clarification and 4 for recap. This is a facilitation example, not a completeness promise. At the time limit, offer an agreed extension, pause or follow-up, and retain uncovered topics explicitly.

## Question policy

Plan around process purpose, trigger, actors, inputs, sequence, systems, decisions, controls, outputs, hand-offs, exceptions, variations, effective dates and evidence owner. Ask about a concrete instance when abstract descriptions omit tacit decisions. Distinguish “the documented policy is,” “we currently do,” “I think,” and “we should change to.”

Use an open prompt before offering alternatives. When choices are helpful, include “both under different conditions,” “neither,” and “I don't know.” Do not turn an uncertain situation into a forced binary choice or anchor the SME to the database answer. Ask what would disprove the apparent conflict.

The system may provide a relevant Atlas fact when the SME asks, but must cite it, explain its scope and retain any refusal/qualification. It cannot fill a knowledge gap with model memory. “The source says X” is different from “X is correct.”

## Challenge and interruption policy

Default to questions at natural pauses. During an initial free account, queue non-urgent discrepancies rather than interrupting. Offer a participant setting for `gentle` (topic boundaries), `balanced` (clear consequential ambiguity at pauses) or `on-request` (summary review). These labels are product proposals and need evaluation.

An unsolicited interruption needs a stable transcript, an identifiable question, relevant evidence or a critical capture problem, and no recent repeated interruption on that point. A configurable cooldown and one pending interruption prevent a barrage. If the participant resumes speaking, stop playback, cancel stale generations and listen. A user “pause,” button press or microphone mute takes precedence over any model plan.

Humour should be optional and light, never directed at a person's competence, accent, memory or mistake. Do not add laughter, sighs or jokes when presenting a disagreement or discussing sensitive information. Silence is acceptable; avoid repetitive filler that hides system delay.

## Example exchanges

These examples are newly written synthetic dialogue, not source quotations or scripts that must be repeated verbatim.

**Uncertain transcription**

SME: “The exception is approved within fifteen days.”

Interviewer: “I may have heard that incorrectly. Was it fifteen or fifty days?”

Outcome: the audio ambiguity is resolved before making a policy-conflict claim.

**Possible scope difference**

SME: “Operations can approve that.”

Interviewer: “Thanks. The standard-process guide lists Finance as the approver. Is the Operations route an exception, a recent change, or a different process?”

Outcome: capture scope and dates; preserve both accounts until the relevant owner reviews them.

**Unknown answer**

SME: “I don't know what happens after the hand-off.”

Interviewer: “That's useful to mark as an open point. Which role would be best placed to explain the next step?”

Outcome: draft a bounded follow-up. Do not claim an invitation was sent or automatically contact the suggested person.

**Delayed check**

Interviewer: “I've captured that. The comparison with the older guidance is still running, so I haven't verified it yet. We can finish the process outline and return to it afterwards.”

Outcome: a late result stays a review item, not an excuse to interrupt repeatedly or lower approval criteria.

**Correction**

SME: “Actually, that changed last month.”

Interviewer: “I'll revise the draft. What changed, and when did the new arrangement take effect?”

Outcome: retain the correction lineage and invalidate earlier review results for the changed claim.

**Second reviewer**

Interviewer: “We have two accounts of the emergency approval route, with different dates. Here are the source passages and the SME-confirmed draft. Which rule applies to this variant, and what authority records the change?”

Outcome: reviewer may approve a scoped rule, request evidence or leave the case disputed. No majority vote or title-based presumption substitutes for domain authority.

## UI proposal

One Atlas page contains session setup, a clear microphone state, captions, the current question, a compact process outline and a review tray. Controls include start/stop, pause, mute, typed input, transcript correction, pronunciation feedback and challenge intensity. Avoid a live numerical judgement of the SME.

During free narration, keep the page calm. Show queued questions without flashing red warnings. In the review tray, each issue has the participant's wording, a source passage, applicability/date information and a clear state: unchecked, possible discrepancy, needs owner review or resolved with rationale. Colour is supplemented by text and icons.

The end-of-session summary separates captured draft, SME-confirmed wording, completed checks, unresolved cases and approved/publication status. Never display “all verified” if any required check failed, timed out, was capped or was skipped.

## Contributor trust and analytics

Explain actual access and retention; do not promise confidentiality or deletion beyond implemented policy. Allow correction, withdrawal requests and marking a point as uncertain. “Off record” must pause capture, not secretly keep a hidden transcript. A later deletion request must identify what can be removed and what governance provenance must be retained under the approved policy.

Measure aggregate interruption appropriateness, ease of correction, fatigue, perceived benefit, review effort and return willingness. Do not score individual honesty, intelligence, employee performance or emotional state. Present a draft of the process as the contributor's reusable output, not a transcript that appears to bind them permanently.
