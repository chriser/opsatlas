# REF S23 #2140: staged publish, red-team rounds (3–4 October 2026)

S23 came out of H3b's sixth round (see `2026-10-03-h3b-stop-rule.md`): a failed publish put the record back at the
same version and fingerprint, so text read in flight passed every check. S23 prepares a new version beside the live
one and puts it in place only once its record is written. Branch `claude/ref-h3b`.

The red team gets the promises (S1–S7) and the code, never the tests or these notes. Each break becomes a hermetic
test in `tests/redteam/test_s23_round<N>_redteam.py`; every fix keeps that test, and the kinds join
`tests/test_scenarios_publish.py`.

## Decisions (the Human)

- **Round 2:** versions are named at commit; every write settles first; the approval event is written once.
- **Round 3:** one writer per space. Exit rule: stop the loop after a round with no break above low, record the lows as
  stated limits, then send H3b and S23 to Codex together.
- **Round 5:** one lock per workspace. Rounds 4 and 5 both found deadlocks between locks taken in different orders, so
  the stop rule applied; the flaw was several locks, and the simpler design is one lock for the whole workspace.

## Round 6

Both breaks were places where the one-lock design had not yet reached, not faults in it:
- the content store still had its own lock, taken before the workspace lock when a suggestion was accepted or
  reopened. It now locks with the space's one lock, so no lock is ever taken before it;
- a reader that met the busy lock between the commit and the move read the new record but the old text. A committed
  version not yet moved is now read where it is staged.

The deadlock is the third round in a row with a fault in locking. If round 7 finds another lock fault, the stop rule
applies again, and the next design has to remove the second path rather than patch it.

## Round 7, and the stop rule

Two breaks, both against S7. Tests: `tests/redteam/test_s23_round7_redteam.py`, strict expected failures.
- **Medium:** reading the content list waits for the workspace lock. Round 6 made the content store's lock the
  workspace lock, and the list takes it to track suggestions. It then holds it through a governance scan, shutting
  out every writer in the workspace while the scan runs.
- **Low by the rubric, but an author's work is lost silently:** drafts, comments, groups and suggestion tracking are
  written without the workspace lock. A draft saved during a publish is wiped by it. Approved text is never affected.

Nothing was found against S1–S6, and no lock-order inversion. Candidates the red team could not test:
- the Sales `published` hook failing after the commit reports the publish as failed (S5);
- a new space's start-up writes before the workspace lock is wired (S7);
- the governance approve route approves the current version without naming the text the reviewer saw (outside S1–S7).

Rounds 4, 5, 6 and 7 all found faults in locking, so the stop rule applies.

### The flaw

The lock is placed by hand: 42 places in 11 files take it, deep inside the stores and services. (First counted as
85 places in 30 files; that count caught every lock in the code, including unrelated in-process ones.) Each round finds a site
missing (round 7: drafts and comments), taken in the wrong order (rounds 4–6), or taken on a read (round 7). One object
also does two jobs: the content store's lock guarded in-process reads until round 6, then became the writer lock.
Correctness rests on every author remembering every site, and no test can show that none is missing.

### The simpler design (proposed): lock at the door, check in the stores

1. **One acquire per write.** Every write request (115 routes: POST, PUT, PATCH, DELETE) takes the workspace lock once,
   at the Sales app's edge, before any handler code runs. Writers that do not come through a request use the same
   wrapper: start-up, a space's creation and restore, and the two background review threads.
2. **Stores check, never take.** Every write function of the register, section store, content store, Sales knowledge
   store and desk refuses to write unless the current request or job holds the lock. A missed path fails loudly in
   any test that reaches it. One test lists every write function and proves that each refuses without the lock.
3. **Reads never take it.** GET requests run without it, and the content store's read consistency gets its own
   in-process lock. Reads that also record something (suggestion tracking; a reader finishing a committed move) only
   try the lock, and skip the recording when it is busy.
4. **The 42 hand-placed sites go.** With one acquire at the outermost point, lock order cannot be wrong.

Unchanged: the staged publish itself (staging, the commit on the record, staged reads, versions named at commit, the
approval event written once).

Costs:
- a slow write (an upload with embeddings, a review) makes other writers in the workspace wait for its whole request;
  readers never wait. Ingest already holds the lock for its whole run today;
- FastAPI runs a handler in a different thread from the app's edge, so "who holds the lock" must follow the request,
  not the thread, and the six async write handlers must not block the server while they wait. Both get tests first;
- about 30 files, mostly removals. S7 is restated as: every write request and job holds the workspace lock from its
  start; stores refuse writes without it; reads never wait.

**Decision (the Human, 4 October 2026):**
- lock at the door, check in the stores, as proposed above;
- a new promise, S8: every approval names the text it approves, and an approval of any other text is refused. This
  covers the governance approve and reject routes, which acted on whatever version was current.

## The door, built (4 October 2026)

- `assistant.storage`: `WriteDoor` (one acquire per request that may change something, at the app's edge; it waits by
  trying, so a queue of writers never ties up the server's threads); `locked` (held by a request or job, re-entrant
  within it, followed into worker threads by a context variable); `if_free` (a reader that would also record
  something takes the lock only if it is free); `writes` / `require` (a governed store refuses a write without it).
- Passes (`DOOR_PASSES`): sign-in and access management, asks and searches, the avatar, Tibi's channel and its proxy,
  service control, the activity log. None writes a governed store; the stores prove it. Tibi's proxy must pass: a
  request held at the door while Tibi writes back would wait for itself.
- The governed stores: each space's register, passages and content store (read-only connection without the lock),
  the Sales knowledge store and the governance desk. A core run on its own is governed by its own door too (its
  register's lock), so its readers' moves are covered as well. Start-up and building a space are jobs.
- The older red-team tests (rounds 1–6 and H3b's) were restated for the door, each saying so; every promise check
  stays. One guard, "a version written without approval is not approved", can no longer be reached by a request (S8
  refuses the approval first); it stays as a second line for jobs, proven by a direct test.
- Readers that wrote: the document view (a first version), the content list (noting suggestions; its scan now holds
  no lock), the library (seeding and placing), a reader's move of a committed version. Each only tries the lock.
- The 42 hand-placed sites are gone. Each store keeps its own in-process lock, taken only by writers, after the door.
- S8: the governance approve and reject routes and the `approve_source` action name the text decided on; the content
  workflow and the Sales review pass it on; the governance page sends the version on screen. A withdrawal by the
  workspace names none.
- S5, round 7's candidate: steps after the commit (the workspace's own records, clearing the draft, the log) no longer
  fail a publish that went live; a failure is noted in the document's activity.

## Round 8 (the door)

The red team found no write path outside the door, no GET that writes, no reader that waits for the lock and no lock
taken before the door's. Three breaks, in approvals, all fixed (tests: `tests/redteam/test_s23_round8_redteam.py`):
- **High:** after a records step failed (which S5 tolerates), a rename built the new version from the record's stale
  copy and wrote it as approved. The title hook now uses the live document's text, and a rename whose body differs
  from the live version's is refused.
- **Medium:** with a file replaced on disk outside content management, a decision naming the shown text approved the
  record's other text. Decisions now read the record's own text (`record_text`); a replaced file is refused until it
  is published as a new version.
- **Low:** a person could reject through the actions route naming no text. Every rejection now names its text, a
  workspace's withdrawal included (under the door it can name the text it withdraws).
- Noted and fixed without a test: a new space's core was reachable by the router an instant before it was governed.

This is a different area from rounds 4–7 (locking), so the stop rule does not apply. The three findings are scenario
kinds in `tests/test_scenarios_publish.py`.

## Round 9, and the stop rule on approvals

Two breaks, both against S8. Tests: `tests/redteam/test_s23_round9_replaced_file.py` and
`test_s23_round9_return_unnamed.py`, strict expected failures.
- **Medium:** a file replaced on disk outside content management can still be approved or rejected through the
  governance route and the `approve_source` / `reject_source` actions: they compare the named SHA-256 with the record's,
  never with the text on disk. Round 8's fix reached the content workflow's decision only.
- **Low:** returning a submitted draft to its author names no text: a reviewer's return, written about one draft, lands
  on a newer one.
- Untested candidate: the Sales check that tells a reworded document from a reformatted one compares against a plain
  read of the file, not the record's text.

Rounds 8 and 9 both found faults in approvals, so the stop rule applies.

### The flaw

A document's approval is written in about seven places (the governance route, the actions' handler, the content
workflow and its fallbacks, the Sales review and its fallbacks, a withdrawal, a move) and guarded by four different
checks that test different things: the text shown, the record's SHA-256, the raw file, the action's rule. Each round
finds a path whose check is missing or tests the wrong thing. It is the locking flaw again, for approvals: a check
placed by hand at every path.

### The simpler design (proposed): decide in the store

1. **One way to change an approval.** The register's `decide(source_id, status, sha)` is the only call that changes a
   document's approval. Under the workspace's lock it checks that the record's text is the one named and that this
   text is what is stored (on disk, or a committed version still staged); otherwise it refuses and nothing changes.
2. **A commit names its own text.** A publish, a rename or a restore sets the approval in the same write that names
   the new text, as now. The register refuses any other write that changes an approval.
3. **Every path calls it.** The governance route, the actions' handler, the content workflow, the Sales review, a
   withdrawal and a move go through `decide`. The per-path checks go; the store's is the one that counts.
4. **Decisions on drafts name the draft.** Publishing already names the draft's SHA-256; returning it to its author
   names it too, in the same one place.
5. **What a document said is the record's text.** Any comparison with what a document said before (the Sales check
   for a reworded document) reads the record's own text, never the file in its place.

**Decision (the Human, 4 October 2026):** decide in the store, as proposed; and decisions on drafts name the draft (a
return to the author names the draft's SHA-256, as publishing does).

### Decide in the store, built

- `SourceRegister.decide(source_id, status, sha)` is the one call that changes an approval. Its check, `names_text`,
  refuses unless the record names that text and the text is what is stored (the live file, or a committed version
  still staged), never a file replaced outside content management. `update` refuses any other change of approval; a
  commit (a write naming a new text) may set it. `withdraw` is the workspace's own fail-closed rejection, reached by
  no route or action.
- Every path calls it: the governance routes and the actions' handler (their early rule asks the register the same
  question), the content workflow and its fallbacks, the Sales review and its fallback. Tests that set an approval
  directly go through `decided` in `tests/door_helpers.py`.
- Decisions on drafts name the draft in one place (`_submitted`): publishing, and returning to the author (the route
  and the document page send the draft's SHA-256).
- The Sales check for a reworded document reads the record's own text.
- The content workflow still checks the record's text first, for a clear message; its guard is retired, since the
  register's check now catches the same case and is the one that counts.

## Round 10, and the stop rule on approvals a third time

No gap in the door (S7) or the staged publish (S1–S6). Two breaks, both against S8, both through the Sales knowledge
store's `_withdraw` (tests: `tests/redteam/test_s23_round10_redteam.py`, strict expected failures):
- **Medium:** a claim's document is published, and the records step after the commit fails (S5 accepts it), so the
  claim's record keeps the old text's hash. A person disputes the claim naming that old hash; the rejection lands on
  the live text, which nobody in the decision named.
- **Low:** with a file replaced on disk, the named rejection is refused, and the withdrawal falls back to the
  workspace's fail-closed `withdraw`, so a person's dispute reaches the one exception.
- Not tested, the same path: a proposal withdrawing an old revision, and a statement finding settled.
- Noted, Low: renaming an approved Sales record records the renamer as its reviewer ("edited and approved").

### The flaw

The Sales knowledge store keeps its own copy of each record's text and hash (about 45 places use it). The copy falls
out of step with the document whenever the records step after a publish fails, which S5 accepts. Decisions in the
Sales layer name the copy's hash and land on the document, and the one exception to `decide` (the fail-closed
withdrawal) is reached from a person's dispute, proposal or settlement, naming whatever text is live.

**Decision (the Human, 4 October 2026):** no exception, and refuse when out of step. The fail-closed withdrawal goes;
disputes, proposals and settlements go through `decide`, naming the text the person named; a Sales decision on a
record whose copy is out of step with its document is refused, and the failed records step is tried again at the next
write to that document.

### No exception, built

- The fail-closed `withdraw` is gone: the register has no way to change an approval but `decide` and a commit.
- The Sales knowledge store's withdrawals (a dispute, a supersession, a corrected proposal, a settled finding) name
  the text the person decided on, the record's hash as they read it; a refusal refuses the whole decision.
- Before any of them acts, every record decided on must be in step with its document (`_in_step`, asking the
  register's `names_text`); otherwise the decision is refused and nothing changes.
- A records step that fails after a commit is marked, and tried again at the next write to the document and when a
  Sales decision finds the record out of step. The retry brings the record's copy in step and records no one's
  approval: a person confirms it again.
- Round 10's two tests pass; direct tests prove the all-or-nothing check and the retry; the publish scenarios gain a
  kind for a records step that fails.

## Stated limits (low)

- Staged passages can be moved against the reader's fingerprint rather than the record's, given two faults and a stale
  reader of the same text (round 5; a strict expected failure).
- A view pairing a record with its text falls back to a plain read after three publishes land inside one read (round
  8, untested).
- A reader holding the record of a source deleted meanwhile gets a file error rather than nothing (round 8, untested).
- A reader can wait for the content store's database while a write commits (milliseconds; SQLite's own lock, not the
  workspace's; round 8, untested).
- The content workflow compares the text shown (decoded or extracted) and approves by the stored bytes: two versions
  whose bytes differ but whose text reads the same both pass (round 10, not a break: readers see the same text).
- Renaming an approved Sales record records the renamer as its reviewer ("edited and approved"; round 10, a label).
