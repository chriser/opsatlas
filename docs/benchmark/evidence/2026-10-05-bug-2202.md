# Bug #2202: any space's owner can lift another space's document restriction (design v2, 5 October 2026)

**How it was found.** The design red team on Bug #2199 (round 2, 5 October 2026, night) found it, with a probe. Tier:
full (access, IAM, REF S13).

**v1, at `14bafda`.** It refused any change to a row held by another space, and checked new rows through a resolver in
the router. Its design red team found 1 High, 4 Mediums and 5 Lows. The report is local:
`research_notes/design-red-team-2026-10-05-bug-2202.md`, with probes in `research_notes/design-red-team-2202-probes/`.

- **The flaw.** v1 tied a row for ever to the space that first wrote it. Rows outlive moves, and nothing deletes them,
  so after a Transfer neither the new space nor the old one could restrict the document (DI1). Today the new space can.
- **v2 takes the red team's simpler rule,** which its probe passes: the row follows the space that holds the resource.

**Round 2 on v2 (`271d9df`): ready to build.** Nothing above Low; 8 Lows. The report is local:
`research_notes/design-red-team-2026-10-05-bug-2202-round2.md`. The Lows went into the build:

- **R2-2:** the update applies only if the row's space is still the one the check saw, and an insert that meets a
  row written meanwhile answers 409. The resolver stays outside IAM's transaction.
- **R2-4:** the Sales app sets the workspace resolver after its cores exist, over the guide core's own-space one.
  It reads the cores live, on the shared instance.
- **R2-5:** an unreadable store, or an unknown space, counts as "cannot tell", which refuses the change.
- **R2-6:** the inventory test scans for write verbs on the table and for store calls, and checks with a
  recording resolver that the writer consults it.
- **R2-7:** the seeded scenario suite runs at IAM level, against a separate statement of the rule. (b) has a
  guard of its own.
- **R2-1, R2-3:** the held-twice limit and the moves limit are worded as they behave (below).
- **R2-8, records:**
  - the cross-space group Bug is #2205;
  - the IAM guide is updated;
  - the guard entries are a change to `tests/guard_register.py`, not a new file;
  - the go-live includes a read-only check of the live `iam.db` for misfiled rows.

**Code red team, round 1 (`dbf848e`): not ready.** It found no way for a space that does not hold a resource to change
its restriction, but 1 Medium and 6 Lows. The report is local: `research_notes/code-red-team-2026-10-05-bug-2202.md`.

  - On main those requests succeed, so it broke I3.
  - Now an archived space holds nothing. It serves no one, and the policy refuses everything done in it except
    restoring it. IAM answers that itself, before asking the resolver.
  - Three tests and a guard cover it.
- **CRT-2, fixed:** the inventory test now works per function, not per file, and catches the forms the probe found:

  inside IAM's service. A planted test proves each form is caught.
- **CRT-3, fixed:** a failing resolver is logged before its change is refused. An insert's integrity error is a 409

- **CRT-4, fixed:** the P-1 test, the 409 test and every restriction attempt in the scenario suite check that a
  refusal or a 409 leaves no audit event and no policy-version change; the no-resolver test checks the audit trail.
- **CRT-5, fixed:** four more guards, each proven: the archived rule, the conditional write, and the two resolvers,
  which are now module functions so they can be switched off. The no-resolver test joins the first guard's proof.
- **CRT-6, records:** corrected below and in the IAM guide.
- **CRT-7, stated:** an older behaviour, below.

**Code red team, round 2 (`10327f5`): ready for the independent review.** Nothing above Low; 5 Lows. No space that does

## What happens today

- **The table.** IAM keeps one row per restricted document or folder in `resource_policies`, keyed by
  `(resource_type, resource_id)` only, with a `space_id` column.
- **The one writer.** `Identity.restrict` (`src/assistant/iam/service.py`) checks `resources.permissions.manage` in the
  space named in the request's path. It then updates or inserts the row for that resource, rewriting `space_id` to the
  caller's space, and never checks that the resource is in that space.
- **The readers.** Both read by space: `restrictions(space_id)` and the policy's resource check (`policy.py`). So a row
  moved to the caller's space stops applying where the resource is.
- **The effect.** Acme's owner lifted a restricted Product Guide document's restriction from Acme's path (200), and a
  guide reader then read it.

## Promises (v2)

- **I1, one rule, in the one writer.** `Identity.restrict` writes a resource's row only for a space that holds the
  resource now:
  - **(a)** A space that holds the resource may set, change or lift its restriction. The row follows it: today's rewrite
    of the row's space, now behind this check. A row misfiled before the fix is taken back the same way, by the space
    that holds the resource.
  - **(b)** A space that holds the resource may not take the row from another space that also still holds it, as with a
    document held twice (Bug #2186).
  - **(c)** A space that does not hold the resource is refused, except that the row's own space may still change it when
    no space holds the resource any more, to lift a deleted document's restriction.

  A refusal is 404, as for a resource that does not exist, with nothing changed and no audit event.
- **I2, the resolver is required.** `holds(space_id, resource_type, resource_id)` is set on the `Identity` that the
  app's cores share:
  - a lone core: its own space;
  - the Sales workspace: its cores, read live, so a space created at run time is known.

  With no resolver, `restrict` refuses. An inventory test proves that `restrict` is the only writer of `resource_policies`,
  through its conditional update `_move_row`, which nothing else reaches, and that it always consults the resolver.
- **I3, nothing else changes.**
  - A space's owner restricting their own space's document or folder works exactly as today.
  - After a Transfer, the new space can restrict the document, as today, and the old space cannot.
  - Reading and evaluating restrictions are unchanged.
  - An unknown resource type still answers 400.

## Design

- **`Identity.holds`,** a callable, defaults to None. `create_app` sets it when absent: its register for documents, its
  content store's `groups()` for folders (read-only; not `library()`, which may write). The Sales app sets the workspace
  resolver once, on the shared `Identity`, reading `app.state.cores` live.
- **`Identity.restrict`** applies I1 before its transaction, then writes as today. It checks the type first, so an
  unknown type keeps its 400.
- **The route** is unchanged. The panel's one caller already puts the active space in both the path and the header.
- **An archived space holds nothing (CRT-1).** IAM answers that from its own record of the space, before asking the
  resolver. Nothing can be done in an archived space anyway: the policy refuses it (404).
- **Seven guards,** each proven: I1's check, (b), the archived rule, no resolver, the conditional write, and each
  resolver (DI3).

## The failure list

- **A document moved between the check and the write** (`/api/iam` passes the workspace's door). It is a moment's
  window: the row can follow a space that no longer holds the document. The holder can take it back. Stated.
- **Duplicate or stale input:** each PUT is judged on its own against the holders now.
- **Two requests at once:** IAM writes in one transaction.
- **A crash, a restart, a failed write:** one transaction; a refusal writes nothing.
- **The evidence changing underneath:**
  - a document deleted after its restriction was set: its own space can still lift it (c);
  - a document moved: it arrives unrestricted in the new space (moving does not move its restriction, Bug
    #2203) until the new space restricts it, which takes the row (a);
  - a document held twice: the row's space keeps it (b).
- **A dependency down:** a change now reads the holding space's store, and for (b) and (c) the other spaces'. A store
  that cannot be read makes its space "cannot tell": the failure is logged and the change refused (404). Nothing is
  written.
- **A space being restored or created (CRT2-2):** IAM records the space as active just before its core is built.
  - In that moment (about 0.06 s in the tests), a row filed under it blocks the space that now holds the document: 404,
    where main answers 200. It fails closed.
  - If the restore or creation fails after IAM's record, the block lasts until a restart.
- **Date and time, model output:** not involved.

## Tests

All in `tests/test_restriction_space.py`: 20 pass, the scenario suite among them. Run against main without the fix,
13 fail. The 7 that pass there:

- I3's behaviours: the own space as before, a request served by another core, a misfiled row taken back, and CRT-1's
  three cases (an archived origin, a misfiled row under an archived space, a deleted document's lift);
- the planted test of the inventory's scanner, which does not depend on the fix.

- **Named tests:**
  - the P-1 probe, which fails before the fix;
  - a new row from a space that does not hold the resource;
  - another space's folder, and a non-empty audience from another space;
  - the owner's own document, set, changed and lifted, with today's audit events;
  - after a Transfer: the new space succeeds, the old one gets 404;
  - a misfiled row taken back by the holder;
  - a deleted document's, and a deleted folder's, restriction lifted by its own space only;
  - a document held twice;
  - a request served by a core other than the path's space;
  - a space created at run time (the workspace fixture creates Acme after start);
  - an archived origin after a Transfer, a misfiled row under an archived space, and a deleted document's lift while
    a space is archived (CRT-1);
  - a lone core: its own space's documents and folders only;
  - a resolver that answers None, or fails: refused;
  - a row written, or moved, between the check and the write: 409, nothing changed;
  - no resolver: refused.
- **The inventory test (I2).**
- **A seeded random-scenario suite** (rule 3) on `tests/scenarios.py`, at IAM level: 2,000 runs by default, in about
  two seconds. Each run mixes restrictions, moves, duplicates, deletions, misfiled rows, archiving and restoring, and
  spaces that cannot tell or fail, across three spaces. After every step it checks the row's space. After every
  restriction attempt it also checks the answer, the audit trail and the policy version, against a separate statement
  of the rule written from the design's (a), (b) and (c). That statement follows the code's clauses, so the named
  tests carry the cases. A failure prints the seed that replays it.
- **The inventory's scanner,** proven on planted code (CRT-2, CRT2-1).
- **Seven guards,** proven.

## Stated limits

- **The moment's window** between the check and the write, above.
- **A document held twice:** only the row's space can restrict it. The other copy is open to its own space's
  readers once approved there, and its owner gets 404 when restricting it. Bug #2186 settles the duplicate.
- **An archived space holds nothing.** Its own rows stay while it is archived, since no one else holds its documents.
  If it shares a document with an active space (held twice), the active space can take the row (CRT2-4). On restore:
  - its own copy is open to its readers, as the held-twice limit says;
  - its owner gets 404 when restricting that copy again, since the row now belongs to the space that took it;
  - its audit trail shows nothing, because the take is filed under the taker's space.

  Main is worse: any space could take the row.
- **A resolver that fails** makes its space "cannot tell": logged, and the change refused.
- **The inventory** reads the code's text. A table name built from pieces at run time escapes it, as computed names
  escape S59's rules. Tooling outside `src/` and `services/` is not scanned (the Human's question on the tooling's
  scope). Log or error text that reads like a write is flagged, which fails closed.
- **The taker's audit event** names the previous audience's people (CRT-7). When a holder takes a row from another
  space, the event's "before" lists the old audience's user ids, without the old space, and the taker's owner can read
  it. Main does the same for any space; the fix narrows it to holders. Left for a follow-up.
- **An audience may name another space's group** (DI9). That space's owner then decides who is in it. This predates
  the fix; Bug #2205 closes it, on top of this one.
