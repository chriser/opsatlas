# Bug #2205: a restriction's audience may name another space's group (design v2, built, 5 October 2026)

**How it was found.** The design red team on Bug #2202 found it (finding DI9, 5 October 2026, night), with a probe:
`research_notes/design-red-team-2202-probes/test_design_redteam_2202.py` (local). Tier: full (access, IAM, REF S13).

**It builds on Bug #2202** (branch `claude/bug-2202-restriction-space`). After that fix, only a space that holds a
document or folder writes its restriction, and the audience it writes replaces the old one. This fix narrows what that
audience may name.

**v1, at `5e0bfa1`.** It allowed the path's space's groups and platform groups. Its design red team found 1 Medium and
6 Lows. The report is local: `research_notes/design-red-team-2026-10-05-bug-2205.md`, with probes in
`research_notes/design-red-team-2205-probes/`.

- **The flaw (F1).** v1 allowed platform groups because "only platform administrators" manage their members. That is
  false today. The built-in identity administrator also holds `iam.groups.members.manage` at the platform, and so can
  any custom platform role. Such a person would decide who reads a space's restricted document without administering
  the space, and around the role ceiling that adding a member to a group applies. A space owner cannot even list
  platform groups.
- **v2 takes the red team's narrower rule:** an audience names only the path's space's own groups. Nothing uses platform
  groups in audiences: the panel offers only people, no test names a group in an audience, and the live store has no
  groups.
- **The Lows are answered below:**
  - F2 is a stated limit with its own Bug, #2208;
  - F3, a platform group stored with an empty space, is refused like any other platform group;
  - F4 and F7 are in the failure list and the go-live check;
  - F5 has an inventory test;
  - F6 shapes the scenario suite.
- **A finding outside this design** is Bug #2207: inviting an existing account with a protected role skips the fresh
  password that a grant requires.

**Round 2 on v2 (`a0df5d5`): ready to build.** Nothing above Low; 5 Lows, all about what the design says. Round 1's F1
actors cannot touch a space group at all. The report is local:
`research_notes/design-red-team-2026-10-05-bug-2205-round2.md`. The Lows went into the build:

- **L1, stated:** inside the space, whoever holds `iam.groups.members.manage` there decides a group's members. That can
  be a custom role that does not administer the space. Restricting to a group delegates to them, by the space's own
  role administration. The line is drawn at the space: below, and in the IAM guide.
- **L2:** the F2 limit is restated below with both effects the red team found, and Bug #2208 is updated.
- **L3, changed design:** the inventory cannot tell IAM's `groups` table from the content library's table of the same
  name. So "a group never changes space" is now enforced by IAM's store itself: a trigger refuses any change to a
  group's space or boundary, whatever writes to it.
- **L4:** the scenario suite moves documents (rows follow their holders), archives and restores spaces, uses platform
  groups stored with NULL or `''`, checks audit and policy version on every step, and asserts it reached every path it
  exists for. A folder and case (c) have named tests.
- **L5:** the note for Bug #2203 says that dropping entries must never leave an audience empty, since an empty audience
  means unrestricted.
- **Outside the design:** `invite()` also lacks `grant()`'s self-grant check, so an identity administrator could make
  herself owner of any space. It is added to Bug #2207, which covers the invitation path's missing checks.

## What happens today

- **The audience.** A restriction's audience is a list of entries, `user:<id>` or `group:<id>`. `Identity.restrict`
  (`src/assistant/iam/service.py`) checks only that a named group exists and is not deleted.
- **Groups.** IAM groups belong to one space (`groups.space_id`, boundary `space`) or to the platform (boundary
  `platform`, `space_id` NULL, or `''` when created with an empty space). Only `Identity.create_group` inserts them, and
  only `delete_group` changes them, setting `deleted_at`. A group never changes space.
  - A space group's members are managed with `iam.groups.members.manage` in its space.
  - A platform group's are managed at the platform, by anyone holding that permission there: platform administrators,
    identity administrators, and any custom platform role that carries it.
- **The check.** The policy (`policy.py`, the last step of a decision) lets a reader through a restriction if any of
  their subjects is in the audience. Their subjects are themselves and every group they belong to, in any space.
- **The effect.** A Product Guide document restricted to an Acme group is readable by anyone Acme's owner adds to that
  group. Every active account reads the guide, so Acme's owner decides who reads the restricted guide document.
- **Live data, checked read-only on 5 October 2026 at 02:50:** no restriction rows and no IAM groups.
- **The panel.** The "Who can read" card offers only the space's people, never groups. When it changes an audience, it
  sends back the entries already there, groups included, even those it cannot show (`AccessCard.tsx`).

## Promises

- **I1, one rule, in the one writer.** `Identity.restrict` accepts a `group:<id>` entry only if the group exists, is not
  deleted, and belongs to the path's space (`groups.space_id` equal to it).
  - After Bug #2202 the path's space is the one that holds the resource, or, for a deleted resource, the row's own
    space.
  - Any other group answers as an unknown group does today: 404 "No such group", with nothing changed and no audit
    event. That covers another space's group and every platform group. It does not say whether the group exists
    elsewhere.
- **I2, a row never comes to name a group outside its own space.**
  - Every audience is written through `restrict` (Bug #2202's inventory test), and validated against the row's space
    when written.
  - A group never changes space (an inventory test, below).
  - When a row follows a new holder (Bug #2202 (a)), the holder writes a new audience, validated for its own space.
  - Rows written before the fix are found by a read-only check at go-live.
- **I3, nothing else changes.**
  - People entries are as today.
  - The holding space's own groups are accepted as today.
  - A deleted or unknown group is still 404 "No such group".
  - The policy, the panel, the routes and the response body are unchanged.
  - The one behaviour that changes on purpose: a platform group in an audience, accepted today, is refused. Nothing uses
    one.

## Design

- **`Identity._may_name_group(space_id, group_id) -> bool`:** one read, `SELECT 1 FROM groups WHERE id = ? AND
  deleted_at IS NULL AND space_id = ?`. `restrict`'s audience loop calls it for each group entry, in place of today's
  existence read. A platform group never matches, whether its `space_id` is NULL or `''`, because a path's space is a
  real space id.
- **Order in `restrict` is unchanged:** permission, resource type (400), space (404), audience (this check), then Bug
  #2202's holder rule, then the write. An audience naming another space's group fails before the holder rule, with the
  same 404 an unknown group gets today.
- **`GROUPS_KEEP_THEIR_SPACE`,** in IAM's store (L3): a trigger, created with the schema on every start, that refuses
  any change to a group's `space_id` or `boundary`. Deleting a group, which sets `deleted_at`, still works.
- **Two guards:** with `_may_name_group` switched back to today's existence check, the probe test fails; without the
  trigger, the test that a group never changes space fails.

## The failure list

- **Duplicate or stale input:** each PUT is judged on its own, against the groups now. A duplicate entry is kept once,
  as today.
- **Two requests at once:** a group cannot change space, so the space condition cannot change between the check and the
  write. A group deleted between them leaves an entry that grants no one, because the policy ignores deleted groups'
  members, as today when a named group is deleted later.
- **A crash, a restart, a failed write:** nothing new is stored; a refusal writes nothing.
- **The evidence changing underneath:**
  - a group deleted after it was named: as today, the entry stays and grants no one;
  - a document moved by a Transfer: its row stays where it was (Bug #2203). If #2203 makes rows follow their documents,
    a space group's entry must not travel to another space. #2203's design decides whether to drop such entries or
    refuse, and dropping must never leave an audience empty, since an empty audience means unrestricted (L5).
- **The panel resending what it cannot show (F4):** the card sends back an audience's existing entries when it changes
  it.
  - If one names a deleted group (true today as well), or a group outside the row's space (only possible in a row
    written before the fix), the change answers 404 "No such group".
  - Lifting the restriction still works, since lifting sends an empty audience.
  - None of these rows exist today.
- **A dependency down, date and time, model output:** not involved.

## Tests

All in `tests/test_restriction_audience.py`: 9 pass. Run on Bug #2202's head without this fix, 8 fail. The one that
passes is I3's: the space's own group, accepted, and its member reads.

- **Named tests:**
  - the probe: a guide document's audience naming an Acme group, from the guide's path, is refused (404). The row is
    unchanged, there is no audit event, and once Acme's owner adds a guide reader to the group, she still cannot read
    the document;
  - the holding space's own group: accepted, and its member reads the document;
  - a folder's audience: the same rule;
  - which groups an audience may name: its own space's, yes; another space's, a platform group (NULL or `''`), a
    deleted group and an unknown one, 404 "No such group" with no audit event and no policy change; people, as today;
  - another space's group alongside allowed entries: the whole request is refused;
  - a holder taking back a row (Bug #2202 (a)) with the old space's group: refused, and the row is unchanged; with its
    own group, the row follows;
  - case (c), a deleted document's row: changed by its own space with its own group, not another's;
  - a group never changes space: the store refuses a change of space or boundary, and deleting still works.
- **A seeded random-scenario suite** (rule 3) on `tests/scenarios.py`, 2,000 runs by default, at IAM level, with its
  own record of groups and holdings:
  - documents move between three spaces, so rows follow their holders;
  - spaces are archived and restored;
  - every space tries audiences mixing people with live, deleted and foreign groups, platform groups (NULL or `''`)
    and unknown ones.

  After every step it checks:
  - the answer and its message (no such group, not held, no such space), against an independent statement of both
    rules;
  - that the stored audience is exactly the accepted one;
  - that a refusal leaves no audit event or policy change;
  - I2: no row names a group outside its own space.

  It asserts that it reached every path it exists for, a row following a new holder with groups in its audience among
  them.
- **Two guards,** proven.

## Go-live check (F7)

- **When:** with the core stopped, so nothing is written between the check and the switch.
- **What it reads:** the live `iam.db`, opened read-only.
- **What it lists:** every row with a group entry whose group is not of the row's own space. A platform group (`space_id`
  NULL or `''`), another space's group, and a deleted group of another space all count.
- **If it finds any:** the go-live stops for the Human.

## Stated limits

- **Rows written before the fix are not rewritten.** The go-live check finds them. There were none on 5 October 2026.
- **Inside the space, a group's members are decided by whoever holds `iam.groups.members.manage` there (L1).** That
  can be a custom role that does not administer the space. Restricting a document to a group delegates who reads it to
  them, as the space's own role administration chose. The rule draws its line at the space.
- **A role can still be bound to another space's group (F2, L2, Bug #2208).** This is older than this fix. Its effects:
  - Acme's owner binds an Acme role to a guide group. The guide's owner can then no longer add members to her own group
    (403), and its members show on Acme's members page.
  - A guide role carrying `iam.groups.members.manage`, bound to an Acme group, lets that group's members decide who
    reads guide documents restricted to guide groups. The guide's owner cannot list them.
  - An identity administrator binding a platform role to a guide group stops the guide's owner adding to her own
    audience group, and lets her strip a protected role by removing a member.
