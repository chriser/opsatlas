# Bug #2205: a restriction's audience may name another space's group (design v1, 5 October 2026)

**How it was found.** The design red team on Bug #2202 found it (finding DI9, 5 October 2026, night), with a probe:
`research_notes/design-red-team-2202-probes/test_design_redteam_2202.py` (local). Tier: full (access, IAM, REF S13).

**It builds on Bug #2202** (branch `claude/bug-2202-restriction-space`, `dbf848e`). After that fix, only a space that
holds a document or folder writes its restriction, and the audience it writes replaces the old one. This fix narrows
what that audience may name.

## What happens today

- **The audience.** A restriction's audience is a list of entries, `user:<id>` or `group:<id>`. `Identity.restrict`
  (`src/assistant/iam/service.py`) checks only that a named group exists and is not deleted.
- **Groups.** IAM groups belong to one space (`groups.space_id`, boundary `space`) or to the platform (`space_id` NULL,
  boundary `platform`). Only `Identity.create_group` makes them. A group never changes space: there is no update. A
  space group's members are managed with `iam.groups.members.manage` in its space; a platform group's at the platform,
  which only platform administrators hold.
- **The check.** The policy (`policy.py`, the last step of a decision) lets a reader through a restriction if any of
  their subjects is in the audience. Their subjects are themselves and every group they belong to, in any space.
- **The effect.** A Product Guide document restricted to an Acme group is readable by anyone Acme's owner adds to that
  group. Every active account reads the guide, so Acme's owner decides who reads the restricted guide document.
- **Live data, checked read-only on 5 October 2026 at 02:50:** no restriction rows and no IAM groups.
- **The panel.** The "Who can read" card offers only the space's people, never groups. Groups reach an audience only
  through the API.

## Promises

- **I1, one rule, in the one writer.** `Identity.restrict` accepts a `group:<id>` entry only if the group exists, is not
  deleted, and belongs to the path's space or to the platform. After Bug #2202 the path's space is the one that holds
  the resource, or, for a deleted resource, the row's own space. Any other group answers as an unknown group does today: 404 "No such group", with nothing changed and no
  audit event. It does not say whether the group exists elsewhere.
- **I2, a row never names another space's group.**
  - Every audience is written through `restrict` (Bug #2202's inventory test), and validated against the row's space at
    the time of writing.
  - A group never changes space.
  - When a row follows a new holder (Bug #2202 (a)), the holder writes a new audience, validated for its own space.

  So after this fix no row can come to name another space's group. Rows written before it are found by a read-only
  check at go-live.
- **I3, nothing else changes.**
  - People entries are as today.
  - The holding space's own groups, and platform groups, are accepted as today.
  - A deleted or unknown group is still 404 "No such group".
  - The policy, the panel, the routes and the response body are unchanged.

## Design

- **`Identity._may_name_group(space_id, group_id) -> bool`:** one read, `SELECT 1 FROM groups WHERE id = ? AND
  deleted_at IS NULL AND (space_id IS NULL OR space_id = ?)`. `restrict`'s audience loop calls it for each group entry,
  in place of today's existence read.
- **Order in `restrict` is unchanged:** permission, resource type (400), space (404), audience (this check), then Bug
  #2202's holder rule, then the write. An audience naming another space's group fails before the holder rule, with the
  same 404 an unknown group gets today.
- **The guard:** with `_may_name_group` switched back to today's existence check, the probe test fails.

## The failure list

- **Duplicate or stale input:** each PUT is judged on its own, against the groups now.
- **Two requests at once:** a group cannot change space, so the space condition cannot change between the check and the
  write. A group deleted between them leaves an entry that grants no one, because the policy ignores deleted groups'
  members, as today when a named group is deleted later.
- **A crash, a restart, a failed write:** nothing new is stored; a refusal writes nothing.
- **The evidence changing underneath:**
  - a group deleted after it was named: as today, the entry stays and grants no one;
  - a document moved by a Transfer: its row stays where it was (Bug #2203). If #2203 makes rows follow their
    documents, a space group's entry must not travel to another space; #2203's design decides whether to drop such
    entries or refuse.
- **A dependency down, date and time, model output:** not involved.

## Tests

- **Named tests:**
  - the probe: a guide document's audience naming an Acme group, from the guide's path, is refused (404), with no
    row, no audit event, and the Acme member unable to read the document;
  - another space's group alongside an allowed entry: the whole request is refused;
  - the holding space's own group: accepted, and its member reads the document;
  - a platform group: accepted;
  - a deleted group, and an unknown one: 404 as today;
  - people: as today;
  - a holder taking back a row (Bug #2202 (a)) with another space's group: refused, and the row is unchanged.
- **A seeded random-scenario suite** (rule 3) at IAM level. Random audiences mix people, the space's own groups,
  other spaces' groups, platform groups, and deleted and unknown groups, across three spaces. Each try is accepted
  exactly when an independent statement of the rule says so, and the stored row is exactly the accepted audience.
- **The guard,** proven.

## Stated limits

- **Rows written before the fix are not rewritten.** A read-only check at go-live lists any row whose audience names
  a group of another space; if one exists, the go-live stops for the Human. There were none on 5 October 2026.
- **A platform group is allowed in any space's audience.** Its members are managed only by platform administrators,
  who already administer every space.
